Airline guide · non-EU carriers

Emirates, Qatar Airways, Etihad, Singapore Airlines

None of these are EU airlines — which means EU261 applies only on the EU-departing leg of your journey, not the inbound leg into the EU.

Verified · EU261 scope rule · checked Jun 2026 EU261 covers non-EU airlines only when the flight departs from an EU/EEA airport. A flight from Dubai to London on Emirates is NOT covered. A flight from London to Dubai on Emirates IS covered (departs EU/UK). A flight from Paris to Dubai on Emirates IS covered (departs EU). This departure-airport rule is the single most important thing to check before filing.

What this means for common itineraries

If you fly London → Dubai → Sydney on Emirates, the London → Dubai leg qualifies for EU261 if delayed 3+ hours. The Dubai → Sydney leg does not (non-EU departure). If a delay on Dubai → Sydney causes a significant knock-on effect to your whole journey, it's worth checking whether the original disruption started on the EU-departing leg.

These airlines' own compensation processes

For flights departing their home hubs (Dubai, Doha, Abu Dhabi, Singapore) where EU261 doesn't apply, each airline has its own voluntary compensation policies which are generally less generous than EU261 and not legally mandated. Emirates, Qatar, and Etihad all have online compensation claim forms under their Customer Affairs sections. Singapore Airlines is generally regarded as more proactive with voluntary goodwill gestures but has no legal obligation equivalent to EU261 for non-EU departures.
