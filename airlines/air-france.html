Air France flight delayed or cancelled?

Verified · directly from Air France's own legal page · checked Jun 2026 Air France's passenger rights page states: "The European Commission advises passengers to contact the carrier directly without going through a designated complaint center" to avoid paying processing fees to third parties. This is Air France itself citing the EU regulator's own DIY-first guidance — an unusually direct endorsement of claiming directly.

The voucher-vs-cash decision

Worth reading before you file For cancellations specifically, Air France's own page offers a choice between a non-refundable transportation credit voucher (higher face value, e.g. €500 for medium-haul) or lower cash compensation (e.g. €400 for the same distance). The voucher is worth more in headline terms but is non-refundable and only usable on future Air France flights. If you're not certain you'll fly Air France again, the lower-value cash option is more flexible. For straight delays of 3+ hours, fixed EU261 cash amounts apply without this choice.

What to do if Air France wrongly denies your claim

Real example · documented March 2025 A passenger with a documented 4-hour delay received an initial denial from Air France claiming the delay "was less than three hours" — factually incorrect. They replied with screenshots from four separate flight-tracking sources proving the actual delay length, and Air France reversed the decision and paid the full €600 within 48 hours. Lesson: a wrong initial denial isn't final, and flight-tracking screenshots from FlightAware or Flightradar24 are strong, concrete evidence to have ready.

Escalation path

If Air France denies a claim you believe is valid, France's DGAC (Direction générale de l'aviation civile) is the national enforcement body — free to contact, relevant for flights departing France. The Tourism and Travel Mediation Office (Médiation Tourisme et Voyage) is also an option for out-of-court resolution, and is referenced directly on Air France's own claims page.
