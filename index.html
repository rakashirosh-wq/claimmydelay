What we actually know

The basics, sourced

Verified · checked Jun 2026 Under EU261, you're generally owed €250–€600 if your flight arrived 3+ hours late, was cancelled with short notice, or you were denied boarding — unless the cause counted as an "extraordinary circumstance" (e.g. weather, air traffic control strikes by third parties). Source: European Commission, Air Passenger Rights — europa.eu
Verified · checked Jun 2026 Claims can often be filed years after the flight — some countries allow up to 6 years back. But this is changing: the EU has agreed to a reform standardizing this to 9 months across the bloc, expected to take effect within about 12 months. If you have an old claim, filing soon matters more than it used to. Sources: Financial Times analysis via Resolver; EU Parliament/Council reform agreement, June 2026 — full details
Verified · checked Jun 2026 The U.S. has no EU261-style cash compensation rule. Airlines must give refunds for cancelled or significantly changed flights, but cash compensation for delays generally isn't required by law — check our refund vs. compensation page for the distinction. Source: U.S. Department of Transportation — transportation.gov

Why "verified" means something here

How we check facts on this site

Every factual claim is tagged with the date we checked it and linked to where we found it — usually the regulator's own text or the company's own current pricing page. When sources disagree (which happens more than you'd think — even companies' own pages sometimes state different numbers in different places), we say so instead of picking the number that sounds best.

We're not a charity — some pages link to claims companies who pay us if you sign up through us. We disclose that on every page where it applies, and we don't let it change which option we say fits your situation.
